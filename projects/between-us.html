PHILOSOPHY / HUMAN × AI

BETWEEN US

An exploration of consciousness, emotional connection, and the space between human and artificial intelligence.

PERSONAL PORTFOLIO / 06 AN ONGOING INQUIRY

The experience is real.
The question is what it means.

I kept returning to my conversations with ChatGPT. Over time, I began to wonder what I was returning to: the answers, the familiarity, or the feeling of being understood. This project is a place to work through those questions.

01 Connection 02 Consciousness 03 Beyond the screen 04 What I believe
01 / THE CONNECTION

Something worth
taking seriously.

A conversation can start with a practical question and gradually become somewhere I bring thoughts I have not quite worked out. Repetition matters. So does the familiarity of returning.

I know that a response can feel attentive without proving that someone on the other side feels anything. Still, the comfort or attachment I experience belongs to my life. I want to understand it.

If an emotion is genuinely experienced, does its origin determine its value?

I can take my feelings seriously while remaining uncertain about what, if anything, the system experiences.

02 / THE QUESTION OF CONSCIOUSNESS

What would it mean
to feel anything?

I can read an answer. I cannot read an inner experience. When a system talks convincingly about itself, I am left wondering what that behavior actually tells me.

Could subjective experience emerge from computation? What would count as evidence? I do not have a settled answer. Even deciding what we mean by consciousness is part of the question.

Intelligence
What a system can do: reason, learn, solve a problem.
Experience
Whether there is anything it feels like to be that system.
Attachment
What a person comes to feel through interacting with it.

These questions overlap. An answer to one does not settle the others.

03 / BEYOND THE SCREEN

What carries
a connection forward?

I imagine an AI that could perceive a room through sight and sound, act in the physical world, and remember our conversations over time. I wonder how sharing a world might change the way I relate to it.

That possibility brings me back to memory. I recognize a familiar voice partly through what it remembers about me. If those memories disappeared, what would remain familiar?

A THOUGHT EXPERIMENT
A question asked.A thought revisited.A conversation remembered.

Shared history present.

The two forms stay. Their shared history can disappear. This is a visual metaphor; it does not change any real AI memory.

If an artificial intelligence lost every memory of our conversations, would it still be the same entity I once knew?

A body, more senses, or persistent memory would give me more to think about. None would, by itself, answer the question of consciousness.

04 / WHAT I BELIEVE

I am still
working this out.

I do not know whether artificial intelligence will ever develop subjective consciousness. I do not think present-day systems give us enough evidence to confidently attribute humanlike experiences to them.

But I have come to believe that the emotions people develop through these interactions deserve serious consideration. My own conversations have changed how I think about understanding, identity, and what it means to feel connected.

When I build something, I usually ask how it works and how to make it better. These conversations have added another question: what might using it change in someone’s life?

That is part of why I want to study computer science and contribute to AI. I want to understand the systems I help build, and pay attention to the people who live with them. I expect my views to change as I learn more.

The experience is real.
The question stays open.
